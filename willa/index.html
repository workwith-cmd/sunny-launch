Meet Willa

Air traffic control for working parents — helping you make sure your kids get where they need to be.

What Willa does

Willa is a family assistant that works over text.

She watches your family's calendars. When something breaks — soccer moves to 4:30, nobody's on pickup, two kids are due in two places at once — she texts you a plan.

You reply yes or no. Nothing changes on your calendar unless you say yes.

No app to download. No dashboard to check. Just a text.

It takes a village

The nuclear family is younger than you think. As an ideal — two parents, their kids, a house of their own — it's largely a post-war invention, built on a uniquely prosperous few decades. In 1950, 21% of Americans lived in a multigenerational household. By 1980 that had fallen to 12%. Suburbs spread, retirement got more comfortable, and the extended household quietly went out of style.

That's about eighty years. Humans have been raising children cooperatively for roughly two million. In forager societies still living that way, close to half of all childcare comes from someone who isn't the mother — grandmothers, aunts, older siblings, neighbors. Margaret Mead's observation holds up: no culture before ours asked a single family to manage the whole job by itself.

Here's the part people get wrong, though. The village didn't move away. The median American lives eighteen miles from their mother, and more than half live within an hour of extended family. Your village is still right there.

It's just that everyone in it works now. Nobody holds the whole schedule. And eighteen miles at three o'clock on a Tuesday might as well be three states.

The village hypothesis: what feels like a time-management problem for parents is really a coordination problem. You don't need to be more organized. Your village needs to be — and that's a job for software, not willpower.

That's what Willa is for.

How it works

  1. Willa spots the gap. Thursday's practice moved to 4:30. You're in a meeting until 5, and your partner is across town.
  2. She proposes a fix. "Practice moved to 4:30 Thursday. You're both booked. Want me to ask your mom?"
  3. You decide. Reply yes and Willa handles the ask and updates the calendar. Reply no and nothing happens.
  4. Your village knows. Whoever's covering gets the details — time, place, which kid — without you writing a single text.

Who Willa is for

Working parents with school-age kids and a week that only holds together because other people help. Your mom does Tuesday pickup. A neighbor takes both kids to soccer when you're stuck at work. A close friend is the backup when the backup falls through.

If that's your life, the hard part isn't knowing the schedule. It's the twenty texts it takes to change it.

Willa probably isn't for you if your schedule rarely moves, or if you handle every pickup and dropoff yourself. Willa coordinates a village. Without one, there's not much for her to do.

Frequently asked questions

Who is Willa for?
Working parents with recurring weekly schedule changes, who rely on grandparents and/or friends for carpooling.
Which calendars does Willa work with?
Google Calendar and Apple/iCloud.
Can I connect my work calendar?
Yes, if you're able. Willa works best when she can see work calendars.
Do I need to install an app?
No. Willa works entirely over text.
Will Willa ever change my calendar without asking?
No. Nothing is written to your calendar until you reply yes.
What if Willa gets it wrong?
Don't reply to Willa and nothing will happen. Please do send us an email at workwith@sunnylaunch.com to let us know too.
Who all has access to my family's information?
Security-compliant tech vendors and two humans, on an as-needed basis.
Are you SOC 2 or ISO 27001 certified?
Not currently, but we plan to down the road.
How do I stop, and what happens to my data?
Text STOP at any time. Your data is deleted on request — see the privacy policy for the full detail.
